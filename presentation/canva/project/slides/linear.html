Where our VQC beats the linear model, and where it doesn’t

How the linear score is made

Learn 4 weights and 1 offset. Multiply the 4 inputs by the weights, add up, convert to a score.

How our score is made

Turn each of 4 qubits by angles that depend on the inputs, 4 times, then average the 4 readings.

Speaking time: 4:20 to 5:00. SAY: The linear model learns one weight per input plus an offset. Our circuit turns each qubit by angles computed from the inputs, four times, and averages the four readings, which gives it curved decision boundaries. With noise off, linear scores 0.703, the original VQC 0.580 and ours 0.735, and ours keeps 0.735 under noise. Honestly: at a single alert threshold the linear model still has the higher F1, 0.791 against 0.763; the optional ranking loss closes that gap. And linear needs no quantum hardware at all, so we claim robustness and cost, not quantum advantage. Source: results/raw.
